# Forty synthetic EV charging behaviour interviews

> **SYNTHETIC · METHOD DEVELOPMENT ONLY.** Every speaker and answer in this file is invented. No participant was interviewed or consented, and no audio was transcribed. These examples must not be included in real participant counts, numerical parameter estimates, thesis findings, or attributed participant quotations.

These are complete Q1–Q20 **scripted interview examples** rather than recordings of actual 25–35-minute conversations. They explore possible explanations and conditional decisions. The Google Form and public charging-session records are separate sources; no survey responses or app logs were generated here. Some hypothetical edge cases, including a pilot electric shuttle fleet, are included to test analytic boundaries rather than assert their prevalence in Sri Lanka.

## S001 — SYNTHETIC

**Fictional situation:** Home charger, registered TOU tariff, office commute; occasional public charging  
**Charging contexts discussed:** T2; T1

**Q1. Interviewer:** Having read or heard the participant information, do you voluntarily agree to take part in this interview and allow your responses to be used for the research as explained?

**S001 (fictional):** Yes. I understand this is a mock interview for practice.

**Q2. Interviewer:** Please talk me through how you decide when and where to charge during a normal week.

**S001 (fictional):** On workdays I usually come home and connect the car. I check what I need to do the next morning, then decide whether to schedule the charging for later. If I have errands after work, I sometimes use a charger near the office.

**Q3. Interviewer:** When you have more than one charging option available, what makes you choose one over another?

**S001 (fictional):** Home is easier and normally costs less, so I choose it when the car can stay there overnight. Public charging is useful if I have to continue driving before I get home; I would not go there just for a small top-up.

**Q4. Interviewer:** How do you decide that it is time to charge your vehicle?

**S001 (fictional):** I look at the remaining battery and tomorrow’s journey together. I can let it drop lower for an ordinary office day, but I charge earlier if someone in the family might need the car unexpectedly.

**Q5. Interviewer:** How do you decide when to stop charging?

**S001 (fictional):** I usually set a target that covers the next day with some extra room. I do not always fill it completely; before a longer journey I set a higher target.

**Q6. Interviewer:** If you charge at home, how do you decide whether to start charging immediately or later?

**S001 (fictional):** I connect it when I get home, but I set the car to begin charging later. If I must leave early the next morning, I sometimes start immediately because I would rather pay more than risk having too little charge.

**Q7. Interviewer:** If you have a Time-of-Use electricity tariff, how does it affect your charging decisions?

**S001 (fictional):** We have a TOU tariff, and I try to use the cheaper hours. That depends on the next-day schedule. If I need the car early or forget to set the timer, the charging may start earlier.

**Q8. Interviewer:** When using public charging, how do you choose the station and charger type?

**S001 (fictional):** For public charging I check whether the station is on my route and whether I can do something nearby. I prefer a faster charger if I am travelling; near work I can accept a slower one if I will be parked for a while.

**Q9. Interviewer:** What do you normally do if your preferred public charger is occupied, unavailable or not working?

**S001 (fictional):** First I look at the battery and the time. If I can get home comfortably, I leave; otherwise I check a second station or wait a short time. I do not assume the next station is free.

**Q10. Interviewer:** When planning a long-distance journey, how do you decide where to stop for charging and how much to charge at each stop?

**S001 (fictional):** I look for stops on the route before leaving and keep an alternative in mind. I want enough charge to reach the following reliable station, with a reserve, rather than aiming for the same battery percentage each time.

**Q11. Interviewer:** When visiting a hotel, restaurant, tourist attraction or another leisure destination, how does charging fit into your visit?

**S001 (fictional):** I have charged at a restaurant once when it was convenient. The visit was already planned; the charger did not make us stay longer, and I left once we were ready.

**Q12. Interviewer:** How, if at all, do your charging decisions differ between weekdays, weekends and unusual trips?

**S001 (fictional):** On weekdays I connect after work. On weekends the car may be away during the afternoon and I charge later in the evening. An unexpected family trip can move charging into the daytime.

**Q13. Interviewer:** If electricity or public charging were cheaper during certain hours, how would you decide whether to change your charging time?

**S001 (fictional):** A lower night price matters if the car can stay at home. A discount at a public station would have to be large enough to justify travelling there and waiting; I would not delay a necessary trip for it.

**Q14. Interviewer:** When you charge while doing something else, how do you decide when to return and disconnect the vehicle?

**S001 (fictional):** At home the car can remain connected after it finishes, but it stops drawing power at the target. At a public station I watch the app and return around completion because someone else may need the charger.

**Q15. Interviewer:** When you are unsure about your next journey or whether a charger will be available, how does that uncertainty affect your charging decision?

**S001 (fictional):** If I do not know whether there will be an extra trip tomorrow, I charge earlier and aim a little higher. That is different from a normal office night when I know the distance.

**Q16. Interviewer:** Can you describe a recent occasion when you charged even though you had not planned to?

**S001 (fictional):** Last month a relative needed a lift that evening. I used a public charger near the hospital because the planned overnight home charge would have been too late. I charged less at home the following night.

**Q17. Interviewer:** What, if anything, do you need to coordinate with other people or equipment before you can charge?

**S001 (fictional):** My spouse and I share the vehicle, so I ask about the next morning’s plans. We also try not to run the charger with several heavy household appliances at once.

**Q18. Interviewer:** Since you began using an EV, what has changed in the way you charge, and what caused those changes?

**S001 (fictional):** At first I charged almost every time I came home. After learning my commute, I became more comfortable waiting and using the scheduled start. The occasional urgent trip still changes that habit.

**Q19. Interviewer:** What are the main difficulties you currently experience when charging your EV in Sri Lanka?

**S001 (fictional):** The biggest issue is uncertainty about whether a public charger will work or be free. I keep a second location in mind rather than arriving with almost no battery.

**Q20. Interviewer:** Is there anything else that influences your charging decisions that we have not discussed?

**S001 (fictional):** The decision is mostly about the next use of the car. Price influences the timing, but it does not overrule a family journey.

## S002 — SYNTHETIC

**Fictional situation:** Apartment resident without home charging; urban ride-hailing driver  
**Charging contexts discussed:** T1; occasional T4

**Q1. Interviewer:** Having read or heard the participant information, do you voluntarily agree to take part in this interview and allow your responses to be used for the research as explained?

**S002 (fictional):** Yes. I understand this is a fictional practice interview.

**Q2. Interviewer:** Please talk me through how you decide when and where to charge during a normal week.

**S002 (fictional):** I drive for fares most days. I check my battery before accepting a longer trip and try to charge when demand for rides is quieter. I do not have a charger at my apartment.

**Q3. Interviewer:** When you have more than one charging option available, what makes you choose one over another?

**S002 (fictional):** My choices are public stations, so I compare their location, queues and whether a fast charger is available. A cheaper slow charger is not useful if it keeps me away from work for hours.

**Q4. Interviewer:** How do you decide that it is time to charge your vehicle?

**S002 (fictional):** I start looking before the battery gets very low, especially if a passenger may request an airport trip. The level at which I stop working to charge depends on the jobs coming in, not one fixed number.

**Q5. Interviewer:** How do you decide when to stop charging?

**S002 (fictional):** I stop when I have enough for the next group of trips. On a busy evening I may leave sooner and charge again later; if I am taking a meal break I can wait longer.

**Q6. Interviewer:** If you charge at home, how do you decide whether to start charging immediately or later?

**S002 (fictional):** I cannot charge at home. I park in a shared apartment area and there is no approved charging point there.

**Q7. Interviewer:** If you have a Time-of-Use electricity tariff, how does it affect your charging decisions?

**S002 (fictional):** I do not have a household TOU charging option. A cheaper public tariff could help, but I would also count the income lost while waiting.

**Q8. Interviewer:** When using public charging, how do you choose the station and charger type?

**S002 (fictional):** I use a station that is close to the area where I expect my next ride. I usually choose DC because my car is part of my work. App status is helpful, although it does not always match what I find when I arrive.

**Q9. Interviewer:** What do you normally do if your preferred public charger is occupied, unavailable or not working?

**S002 (fictional):** I call or check another station if the first one is occupied. If I have enough battery I may take a short fare on the way. If the battery is low, I wait rather than risk going too far.

**Q10. Interviewer:** When planning a long-distance journey, how do you decide where to stop for charging and how much to charge at each stop?

**S002 (fictional):** I do not often plan long journeys, but for an airport or intercity booking I check charging options before accepting it. I want a reserve for the return trip, especially if the passenger’s plans change.

**Q11. Interviewer:** When visiting a hotel, restaurant, tourist attraction or another leisure destination, how does charging fit into your visit?

**S002 (fictional):** If a passenger stops at a restaurant I might charge only if there is a suitable charger and enough time. Most such stops are too short, so I do not rely on them.

**Q12. Interviewer:** How, if at all, do your charging decisions differ between weekdays, weekends and unusual trips?

**S002 (fictional):** Weekday ride demand changes by hour, and weekends can be late at night. I try to move a charging break to a quiet work period, but sometimes a good job makes me postpone it.

**Q13. Interviewer:** If electricity or public charging were cheaper during certain hours, how would you decide whether to change your charging time?

**S002 (fictional):** I would shift to a cheaper hour only if the saving exceeded lost driving time and the charger was likely to be free. A price sign alone would not tell me that.

**Q14. Interviewer:** When you charge while doing something else, how do you decide when to return and disconnect the vehicle?

**S002 (fictional):** I use the app and go back when charging is close to done. If a passenger request comes before I reach my target, I decide whether I already have enough battery for that job.

**Q15. Interviewer:** When you are unsure about your next journey or whether a charger will be available, how does that uncertainty affect your charging decision?

**S002 (fictional):** If I am not sure where the next passenger will go, I keep a bigger reserve. If the charging app looks unreliable, I also choose a station with another option nearby.

**Q16. Interviewer:** Can you describe a recent occasion when you charged even though you had not planned to?

**S002 (fictional):** I once accepted an airport ride after planning to finish work. I made an extra short charge before pickup so I would not have to delay the passenger. It meant my usual evening charging stop was shorter.

**Q17. Interviewer:** What, if anything, do you need to coordinate with other people or equipment before you can charge?

**S002 (fictional):** I sometimes need to coordinate the vehicle with another driver in my family. We tell each other the battery level and when the car will be available, but no one can plug it in at home.

**Q18. Interviewer:** Since you began using an EV, what has changed in the way you charge, and what caused those changes?

**S002 (fictional):** At first I waited until the battery was quite low. After finding a charger out of service twice, I began checking availability earlier and keeping a backup stop.

**Q19. Interviewer:** What are the main difficulties you currently experience when charging your EV in Sri Lanka?

**S002 (fictional):** Queues and unreliable station information cost me work time. The physical charger may be there, but I cannot plan around it if it is blocked or broken.

**Q20. Interviewer:** Is there anything else that influences your charging decisions that we have not discussed?

**S002 (fictional):** For me time is a cost. A charging price comparison should include the time I cannot take rides.

**Q17. Interviewer:** What, if anything, do you need to coordinate with other people or equipment before you can charge?

**S003 (fictional):** The family agrees on meal and rest stops, so the charging stop has to fit that. At home we share one parking position with another car, which sometimes changes when I can plug in.

**Q18. Interviewer:** Since you began using an EV, what has changed in the way you charge, and what caused those changes?

**Q19. Interviewer:** What are the main difficulties you currently experience when charging your EV in Sri Lanka?

**S003 (fictional):** A map may show a destination charger without saying whether it is reserved for guests or working. That uncertainty affects both where we stay and how much we charge en route.

**Q20. Interviewer:** Is there anything else that influences your charging decisions that we have not discussed?

**S003 (fictional):** A hotel listing saying “EV charger” is not enough for planning. Whether I can use it at the time I arrive is the important part.
